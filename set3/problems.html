Linear Algebra – Problem Set 3

Linear Independence, Bases & Dimension · 12 problems · Original problems in the spirit of MIT 18.06 (Strang)

Three ideas carry the whole set. (i) Independence is a question about solutions: $v_1,\dots,v_k$ are dependent exactly when $c_1v_1+\cdots+c_kv_k = 0$ has a solution with some $c_i \ne 0$ — that is a homogeneous system $Ac = 0$, so row reduction (Set 2) decides it, and for a square $A$ so does $\det A \ne 0$ (Set 1). (ii) Membership in a span is the same machinery, inhomogeneous: $b \in \operatorname{span}(v_1,\dots,v_k)$ iff $Ac = b$ is consistent. (iii) A basis is a maximal independent set inside a span, and its size — the number of pivots — is the dimension. Coordinates are just the solution of $Bc = v$, written in that basis instead of the standard one.

Problem 1

In $\mathbb{R}^3$, decide whether the three vectors are linearly independent, and justify it by elimination rather than by eye:

$$u_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad u_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad u_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

Give the rank, the value of $\det[u_1\;u_2\;u_3]$, and an explicit nontrivial relation among the three vectors.

Problem 2

In $\mathbb{R}^3$ consider

$$u_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad u_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad u_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad u_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

(a) Find a basis of $\operatorname{span}(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)$ — and justify that no smaller set spans it.
(b) Write each of the four vectors in that basis.
(c) Which vector did you have to drop, and what was the relation that made it drop out?

Problem 3

True or false? Give a counterexample or a correction for each false one.

(a) Any four vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ are linearly dependent.
(b) A set that contains the zero vector is linearly independent.
(c) Two nonzero vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ are dependent if and only if one is a scalar multiple of the other.
(d) If $v_1,\dots,v_4$ are linearly independent in $\mathbb{R}^4$, then any three of them are still linearly independent.
(e) Adding a vector to a linearly independent set always keeps it linearly independent.

Problem 4

Let $u = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$, $v = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$, $w = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $z = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

(a) Is $w \in \operatorname{span}(u,v)$?
(b) Is $z \in \operatorname{span}(u,v)$? Decide by solving the system, not by looking.
(c) Is $\{u,v,z\}$ linearly independent? Support your answer with a determinant.

Problem 5

In the plane, let $a = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$ and $b = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

(a) Compute $\det[a\;b]$ and say what it measures geometrically.
(b) What is the area of the triangle with vertices $0,\ a,\ b$?
(c) Explain why any three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ must be linearly dependent — and connect it to the value you computed in (a).

Problem 6

Let $W = \operatorname{span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right\}$.

(a) Find $\dim W$ and a basis for it.
(b) Show that $W = \mathbb{R}^3$.
(c) Now replace the third vector by $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$ and call the new span $W'$. Find $\dim W'$ and express the new vector in terms of the first two.

Problem 7

In $\mathbb{R}^3$ with respect to the basis $B = \left\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right\}$:

(a) First check that $B$ really is a basis, not just three vectors.
(b) Write $v = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$ in that basis, i.e. solve $Bc = v$.
(c) Verify your answer by multiplying back, and then give the formula that writes an arbitrary $v = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}$ in basis $B$ — that is, compute $B^{-1}$.

Problem 8

Let $B$ be the basis from Problem 7 and let $C = \left\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right\}$.

(a) Find the matrix $S$ satisfying $[v]_C = S\,[v]_B$ for every $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
(b) Check your matrix on $w = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$: compute $[w]_B$ and $[w]_C$ separately, then verify $S[w]_B = [w]_C$.
(c) In one sentence, what does $S$ do geometrically? (Hint: it takes coordinates in one basis and returns coordinates in another.)

Problem 9

Identify every polynomial of degree $\le 2$ with its coefficient vector relative to $(1, x, x^2)$ — the polynomial $a + bx + cx^2$ becomes $(a, b, c)$. Let

$$p_1 = 1 + 2x - x^2, \qquad p_2 = 3 + x + 2x^2, \qquad q = 5 - x + 4x^2.$$

(a) Show that $p_1, p_2$ and $p_3 := 4 + 3x + x^2$ are linearly dependent, and identify the relation.
(b) Is $q$ in $\operatorname{span}(p_1,p_2)$?
(c) Find a nonzero polynomial in $\operatorname{span}(p_1,p_2)$ that has a zero at $x = 1$, and check it.

Problem 10

An experiment records a temperature $T$ and a pressure $P$:

$$T = 0,\ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8 \qquad\qquad P = 1.00,\ 2.00,\ 3.00,\ 4.10,\ 4.90.$$

(a) Do all five points lie exactly on a line $P = aT + b$? Decide by rank.
(b) The first three points do lie on a line. Find it.
(c) Use that line on the last two readings: give the residuals $P_{\text{measured}} - P_{\text{model}}$ and the relative error as a percentage.
(d) Why does "three points in the plane always determine a line, five points generally do not" follow from the same counting argument as Problem 11?

Problem 11

Prove that a linearly independent set in $\mathbb{R}^n$ contains at most $n$ vectors. Give two proofs:

(a) By row reduction: put the vectors in as columns and count pivots.
(b) By determinants: use the fact that a homogeneous system with more unknowns than equations cannot have only the trivial solution.

Problem 12

For a square matrix $A$, prove the chain of equivalences

$$\det A \ne 0 \iff \text{the columns of } A \text{ are linearly independent} \iff Ax = 0 \text{ has only the trivial solution} \iff A \text{ is invertible},$$

then use it to analyse the two matrices

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad A' = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

For each, state which of the four conditions holds. For the second one, also give a basis of the solution set of $A'x = 0$.
