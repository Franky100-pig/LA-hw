Linear Algebra – Problem Set 5

Column, Row & Null Spaces · Basis, Dimension, Rank & Nullity · 13 problems · Original problems in the spirit of MIT 18.06 (Strang)

Set 4 asked what the words mean. This set asks what they compute to. Every matrix $A$ carries three subspaces — $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ and $\operatorname{Nul}(A)$ — and the whole machinery of row reduction exists to describe them. One fact organizes everything: row operations preserve the row space and the dependence pattern of the columns, but not the column space itself. That single asymmetry explains why the basis for $\operatorname{Col}(A)$ is taken from the original matrix while the basis for $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ is taken from the reduced one (Problems 9, 10). The set ends with rank–nullity, which converts "shape plus one number" into answers about pivots, independence and solvability (Problems 11–13).

Problem 1

Two sets in $\mathbb{R}^3$ differ by a single number:

$$V = \left\{\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} : x + 2y - z = 0\right\}, \qquad W = \left\{\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} : x + 2y - z = 1\right\}.$$

(a) Apply the subspace test to each, justifying every condition.
(b) Exactly one of them is a subspace. Name the first condition that fails for the other, with a concrete counterexample.
(c) Give a geometric description of each set, and say in one sentence why changing $0$ to $1$ makes the difference between a subspace and a non-subspace.
(d) For the one that is a subspace, find $\dim V$ and a basis.

Problem 2

Let $S = \operatorname{Span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\right\}$ and $T = \operatorname{Span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1\\-1\end{bmatrix}\right\}$, two lines through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

(a) Describe $S + T$ as explicitly as you can, and prove your claim.
(b) Describe $S \cup T$.
(c) Is $S + T$ a subspace? Is $S \cup T$ a subspace? Justify both answers.
(d) Now answer the same two questions for $S = \operatorname{Span}\{(1,1)\}$ and $T = \operatorname{Span}\{(2,2)\}$ — i.e. the same line twice. What general rule about unions of subspaces do your two experiments suggest?

Problem 3

Let $A$ be a $5 \times 3$ matrix. Without knowing any entry of $A$, state the ambient vector space containing each of $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ and $\operatorname{Nul}(A)$, and justify each answer by counting entries.

Then answer, again without knowing the entries: what is the largest possible value of $\operatorname{rank}(A)$, and what is the largest possible value of $\operatorname{nullity}(A)$?

Problem 4

Let $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 0 & 1\\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2\\ 1 & 3 & 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$, and write its columns as $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4$.

(a) Is $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{bmatrix} \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$?
(b) Is $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix}3\\1\\4\end{bmatrix} \in \operatorname{Col}(A)$? If so, exhibit it as a linear combination of the columns.
(c) Is $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\1\\0\end{bmatrix} \in \operatorname{Nul}(A)$?
(d) Is $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\1\\1\end{bmatrix} \in \operatorname{Nul}(A)$?
(e) Find a basis for $\operatorname{Nul}(A)$.

For (a) and (b), justify by comparing ranks; do not guess from the picture.

Problem 5

Let $A = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & 1\\ 1 & 3 & 1\\ 2 & 5 & 1\end{bmatrix}$.

(a) Explain why $\mathbf{v} \in \operatorname{Row}(A) \iff A^T\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{v}$ is consistent, and why that makes the row-space question a consistency question.
(b) Use this to decide whether $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ belongs to $\operatorname{Row}(A)$.
(c) And whether $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ belongs to $\operatorname{Row}(A)$.
(d) Find a basis for $\operatorname{Row}(A)$ and state its dimension.

Problem 6

For each subset of $M_{2\times 2}$ below, decide whether it is a subspace. For those that are, prove it with the subspace test. For those that are not, name the first condition that fails and give an explicit counterexample (actual matrices, with numbers).

(a) The symmetric matrices, $\{A : A^T = A\}$.
(b) The trace-zero matrices, $\{A : \operatorname{tr}(A) = 0\}$.
(c) The invertible matrices.
(d) $\{A : A^2 = 0\}$.
(e) The upper triangular matrices.
(f) For the fixed matrix $B = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1\\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$, the set $\{A : AB = BA\}$ (the matrices that commute with $B$).

Then: how many of the six are subspaces, and what do the failing ones have in common?

Problem 7

Consider the four vectors $w_1 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}$, $w_2 = \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\2\end{bmatrix}$, $w_3 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\3\end{bmatrix}$, $w_4 = \begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\0\end{bmatrix}$, and let $H = \operatorname{Span}\{w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4\}$.

(a) Find a basis for $H$ containing as few of the original vectors as possible.
(b) State $\dim H$.
(c) Prove carefully that your basis still spans all four original vectors — not just the ones you kept. Write out the algebra.
(d) Is $H$ a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$? Can $H = \mathbb{R}^3$? Justify with dimensions.

Problem 8

Let $W = \{p(x) \in P_3 : p(1) = 0\}$, the polynomials of degree at most $3$ that vanish at $x = 1$.

(a) Show $W$ is a subspace of $P_3$.
(b) Write a general polynomial of $W$ using free parameters, and read a basis off that representation.
(c) State $\dim W$ and $\dim P_3$, and check that they differ by exactly the number of conditions imposed.
(d) Is $\{x - 1,\ x^2 - 1\}$ a basis for $W$? Explain in one sentence why not.

Problem 9

Let $A = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 3\\ 4 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 5\\ 1 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 1\\ 3 & 1 & 1 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}$. Row reduction gives

$$R = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 2\\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 & -1\\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & -1\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}.$$

(a) Mark the pivot columns and the free variables.
(b) Find a basis for $\operatorname{Col}(A)$.
(c) Find a basis for $\operatorname{Row}(A)$.
(d) Find a basis for $\operatorname{Nul}(A)$.
(e) State $\operatorname{rank}(A)$ and $\operatorname{nullity}(A)$, and verify rank–nullity.
(f) In part (b), which matrix did you take the vectors from — $A$ or $R$? In part (c)? Explain the asymmetry in one sentence.

Problem 10

A student writes: "After row reducing $A$ to $R$, I should use the pivot columns of $R$ as a basis for $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, since $A$ and $R$ are row equivalent."

(a) Show with a concrete $2 \times 2$ example that $\operatorname{Col}(A) \ne \operatorname{Col}(R)$ in general, so the proposed vectors need not even lie in $\operatorname{Col}(A)$.
(b) Explain what row operations do preserve: the row space, and the dependence pattern of the columns. Prove the second claim using $R = EA$ with $E$ invertible.
(c) Using (b), justify both correct rules: take pivot columns from $A$ for $\operatorname{Col}(A)$, and nonzero rows of $R$ for $\operatorname{Row}(A)$.
(d) Conclude with one sentence stating what row operations preserve and what they do not.

Problem 11

Suppose $A$ is a $6 \times 9$ matrix with $\operatorname{rank}(A) = 4$.

(a) How many pivot columns? (b) How many free variables in $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$? (c) $\dim\operatorname{Col}(A)$? (d) $\dim\operatorname{Row}(A)$? (e) $\dim\operatorname{Nul}(A)$? (f) Can the columns of $A$ be linearly independent? (g) Can $\operatorname{Col}(A) = \mathbb{R}^6$? (h) Must $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ have a solution for every $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^6$? (i) Can $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ have a unique solution for some $\mathbf{b}$?

Answer all nine, then write one paragraph explaining how the answers are connected rather than nine unrelated facts. In particular, identify which two comparisons of $\operatorname{rank}(A)$ against the dimensions $6$ and $9$ decide everything.

Problem 12

Let $v_1 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\1\\0\end{bmatrix}$, $v_2 = \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix}$, $v_3 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\1\\0\end{bmatrix}$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$.

(a) Are $v_1, v_2, v_3$ linearly independent? Justify.
(b) Let $S = \operatorname{Span}\{v_1, v_2, v_3\}$. State $\dim S$.
(c) Extend $\{v_1, v_2, v_3\}$ to a basis of $\mathbb{R}^4$ by adding one standard basis vector. Which standard vectors $e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4$ would work, and which would not?
(d) Explain the general method for extending an independent set to a basis, and why it must terminate.

Problem 13

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix.

(a) Prove that the following four statements are equivalent: (i) $\operatorname{rank}(A) = n$; (ii) $\operatorname{nullity}(A) = 0$; (iii) $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ has only the trivial solution; (iv) $A$ has a pivot in every column.
(b) Explain how these four relate to $A$ being invertible, and why "pivot in every column" is enough for a square matrix when it would not be enough for a rectangular one.
(c) Close the loop with Set 4: explain why all of the above are also equivalent to $\det A \ne 0$ and to the columns of $A$ being linearly independent.
